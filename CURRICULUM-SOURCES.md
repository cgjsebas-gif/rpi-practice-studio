# University-style extension — November 2026

36 original problems were added (v3-1 through v3-36), 12 per course. These are not copied exam questions or representations of RPI exam coverage. The existing learning hubs, grading fields and guidance-first solution controls are retained.

## Reference scope

- [Tennessee Tech: David Murdock, Worked Examples, Vol. I](https://www2.tntech.edu/leap/murdock/books/w1book.pdf): mechanics coverage and reasoning-first worked-example structure. This particular volume is algebra-based; the new bank also includes symbolic calculus-based derivations.
- [Syracuse PHY211](https://suphysics.gitlab.io/phy211/): introductory mechanics course and review resources.
- [Lehman faculty-hosted mechanics problem collection](https://www.lehman.edu/faculty/anchordoqui/SJ1.pdf): mechanics problem style; hosted textbook material is not assumed to be openly licensed and is not reproduced.
- [Paul's Online Math Notes](https://tutorial.math.lamar.edu/Classes/CalcI/LogDiff.aspx) and [calc1.org exam archive](https://www.calc1.org/exams): differentiation and exam-style calculus coverage.
- [WVU CHEM233 resources](https://community.wvu.edu/~josbour1/pages/Chem233.html) and [Jasperse course resources](https://web.mnstate.edu/jasperse/): organic chemistry problem-set organization and topic scope.

## Content and checking

Physics includes component motion, projectile motion, two-mass force balances, friction, spring energy, one- and two-dimensional momentum, loop contact, rod torque and SHM. Every new physics solution includes a free-body/diagram description, symbolic derivation before substitution, and a dimensional check. Existing diagram hooks containing fixed numbers were deliberately not reused with conflicting data.

Calculus covers one-sided continuity, implicit/logarithmic/inverse-trig differentiation, related rates, constrained optimization, MVT, concavity, justified L'Hôpital, substitution and moving-limit FTC.

Organic chemistry uses multipart reasoning and labeled self-assessment rubrics: multifunctional naming, acid-base equilibrium and ARIO, contributors and halogen effects, heteroaromaticity, CIP/isotopes, conformations, meso/enantiomer distinctions, rate orders/Arrhenius, bond energies/free energy and mechanism competition. R/S and erythro/threo do not predict optical rotation. pKa values are approximate and solvent-dependent, so supplied same-scale values are used rather than treating a single universal table as exact.

The curriculum test checks all 36 IDs, course counts, hub membership, solution depth, independently rearranged physical equations, numerical calculus checks and chemistry arithmetic. The existing bank/grade tests check syntax, schema and accepted answers. Open-ended chemistry and symbolic explanations remain rubric-assessed, not automatically judged as chemically equivalent strings.
